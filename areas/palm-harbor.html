What a Palm Harbor kitchen remodel usually includes

A typical Palm Harbor kitchen is cabinet replacement or a full layout shift, new counters, backsplash, lighting, and flooring that continues into the adjacent dining or family room so the remodel does not stop at an ugly transition strip. Soffit removal is common on 1970s stock. So is moving a range or sink a few feet — which is when Pinellas permitting enters the conversation.

We do not sell a catalog kitchen. You pick the boxes and slab, or we coordinate with the supplier you already started with. The JNS piece is field: demo, carpentry, waterproofing at wet walls, punch, and close-out. See finished kitchens in the project gallery — those photos are JNS jobs, not stock interiors from a manufacturer.

If the kitchen is only the first room in a larger plan (bath, laundry, or a small addition), we write it as one scope under project coordination so trades are not stacking change orders against each other.

Bathrooms, additions, and work that failed inspection

Hall baths and primary baths in Palm Harbor fail the same way across this housing stock: leaking pans, rotted blocking, undersized exhaust, and valves that were never meant to last forty years. A walk-in shower conversion or a double-vanity primary bath is licensed remodel work. We waterproof, tile, and close it to a written spec.

When a prior crew left an open inspection or a failed one, that is corrective work, not a paint-over. We document what is wrong, what Pinellas will accept, and what it costs before demo day two.

Room additions, garage conversions, and framing belong on general construction. Palm Harbor lots can take a modest bump-out; we confirm setbacks and the permit path on the site visit, not after the slab is formed.
